Enhancing Chain-of-Zoom by integrating historical prompt context and semantic anchor rewards to prevent semantic drift and detail stagnation in extreme recursive upscaling.

Abstract

Chain-of-Zoom (CoZ) is an autoregressive framework that achieves extreme super-resolution (up to 256x) by decomposing magnification into intermediate scale-states guided by VLM-generated prompts. While CoZ effectively uses VLMs to compensate for sparse visual evidence, it remains vulnerable to semantic drift, where recursive zooming causes the VLM to lose global context and hallucinate incorrect details. Additionally, the model faces information convergence, where repetitive prompts fail to introduce new high-frequency textures at deep scales.

This project proposes to enhance CoZ by expanding its state space to include historical prompts and scale factors. We also introduce a Semantic Anchor Reward via GRPO to ground deep-scale generations in the original image's global label, ensuring semantic stability and continuous information gain throughout the zoom process.

Proposed Methodology

1. Redesigned State Space ($S$)

To prevent disorientation during the recursive process, we augment the state representation $s_t$ by extending the observation beyond the current local image crop $x_{i-1}$:

  • Scale factor information: Provides a quantitative indicator of the current magnification level, helping the agent adjust its descriptions to the right level of detail according to the specific zoom stage.
  • Historical context: By adding the previous scale-state $x_{i-2}$, we provide a trajectory of the zoom process, which ensures better semantic consistency.

2. Redesigned Multi-Objective Reward via GRPO

We employ Generalized Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO) to maximize a multi-objective reward function to align the prompt-extraction VLM:

  • Semantic anchor reward ($R_{anc}$): To fix the semantic drift, we introduce a reward that measures the consistency between the agent's current prompt and the description of the original low-resolution image $x_0$. This acts as a permanent anchor.
  • Repetition penalty ($R_{rep}$): To solve the information stagnation, we apply a penalty for cross-scale repetition, forcing the agent to provide new, high-frequency details.
  • Intermediate SR feedback reward ($R_{fb}$): We introduce a reward based on the actual image output from the previous SR step to encourage the agent to correct visual artifacts.

Empirical Evaluation

Performance Metrics

We evaluate our result using no-reference Visual Quality Assessment (VQA) metrics, specifically NIQE, MUSIQ, MANIQA, and CLIPIQA to measure perceptual image quality. Reference-based metrics (e.g., PSNR, SSIM) are excluded since extreme super-resolution tasks lack high-resolution ground truth images.

Dataset & Baselines

We utilize open-resource datasets including DIV2K (800 images) and DIV8K (1500 images), center-cropped to $512 \times 512$. Our proposed model is benchmarked against:

  • NN Interpolation (Spatial upsampling)
  • Direct SR (Single forward pass)
  • Original Chain-of-Zoom (NeurIPS 2025 Baseline)
